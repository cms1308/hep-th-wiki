## TL;DR

Conjectures that in any consistent quantum-gravity theory "gravity is the weakest force":
for every $U(1)$ gauge field there exist charged states with mass/charge ratio below that
of large extremal black holes, so extremal black holes can decay. This implies a light
charged particle with $m \lesssim g M_{\rm Pl}$ and a hidden UV cutoff
$\Lambda \lesssim g M_{\rm Pl}$ on the effective $U(1)$ theory. The founding paper of the
[[weak-gravity-conjecture|Weak Gravity Conjecture]] and, with Vafa 2005
([hep-th/0509212](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/0509212)), a pillar of the
[[swampland-program]].

## Motivation

An effective field theorist sees nothing wrong with an arbitrarily small $U(1)$ gauge
coupling $g$ — the theory only looks more weakly coupled, with cutoff still $\sim M_{\rm Pl}$
as long as the Landau pole stays above $M_{\rm Pl}$. Yet $g \to 0$ turns the gauge symmetry
into an effectively global one, which quantum gravity is believed to forbid
([[no-global-symmetries-in-quantum-gravity]]). Something must obstruct the limit; the
conjectured cutoff $\Lambda \lesssim g M_{\rm Pl}$ is the proposed obstruction — a new
landscape-vs-swampland criterion extending the finiteness criteria of Vafa 2005 and of
Douglas (Strings 2005 talk). A second
motivation is phenomenological: explaining why natural-looking effective models of
inflation with super-Planckian axion decay constants (e.g. [[extranatural-inflation]])
resist embedding in string theory.

## Historical context

- Vafa 2005 ([hep-th/0509212](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/0509212)) introduced the
  swampland and first criteria (finiteness of massless fields, finite moduli-space volume);
  this paper adds a new criterion.
- The [[black-hole-remnant|remnant]] argument and the large-$N$ species problem
  ([[hep-th-9501106|Susskind 1995]], a thermodynamic Rindler-entropy argument that a remnant
  species drives the renormalized $G\to0$ — it does not itself discuss symmetries) supply the
  heuristic engine for the "same pathology" claim against global symmetries.
- Sen 1995 ([hep-th/9411187](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/9411187)) constructed the
  heterotic extremal black holes (BPS and non-BPS) used in the main quantitative check.
- Banks-Dine-Fox-Gorbatov 2003 ([hep-th/0303252](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/0303252))
  found string theory resists parametrically super-Planckian axion decay constants — later
  subsumed here as the $D=4$, $p=0$ case of the generalized conjecture.
- Positivity of $F^4$ coefficients from causality/analyticity
  (Adams-Arkani-Hamed-Dubovsky-Nicolis-Rattazzi,
  [hep-th/0602178](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/0602178)) connects in Sec. 5.
- Fractionally charged heavy winding states (Wen-Witten 1985, Nucl. Phys. B261, 651) kill
  the minimal-charge form (I) of the conjecture.

## Main results

1. **Loose electric form**: a 4d theory with gravity and a $U(1)$ of coupling $g$ has a
   charged particle with $m_{\rm el} \lesssim g\, M_{\rm Pl}$ (all Sec. 2 bounds are
   parametric $\lesssim$, no $O(1)$ coefficients claimed).
2. **Magnetic form → UV cutoff**: monopoles obey
   $m_{\rm mag} \lesssim M_{\rm Pl}/g_{\rm el}$; since a minimal monopole in a theory with
   cutoff $\Lambda$ has $m_{\rm mag} \sim \Lambda/g^2$, this gives
   $$\Lambda \;\lesssim\; g\, M_{\rm Pl}\,,$$
   "more precisely $\Lambda \sim \sqrt{\alpha/G_N}$" — a UV scale invisible to the
   low-energy effective field theorist. (The "magnetic WGC" of later literature is derived
   here from the monopole bound, not posited independently.) Scale dependence: for the
   light-particle bound, $g$ is the running coupling at the mass of the lightest charged
   particle; for the cutoff bound, $g$ near $\Lambda$.
3. **Sharp forms (I)/(II)/(III)** (units $M_{\rm Pl}=1$, charge normalized so large
   extremal black holes have $M/Q = 1$): (I) the state of minimal charge, (II) the lightest
   charged particle, (III) some state, has $M/q \le 1$. (I) is refuted by the stable heavy
   $SO(32)$ spinors and Wen-Witten fractionally charged states; (II) is the strongest
   surviving form; the evidence mostly supports only (III):
   "Even though we have no counterexamples for conjecture (II) most of our evidence only
   supports the weaker conjecture (III)" ([[wgc-strong-vs-weak-form]]).
4. **Multiple $U(1)$'s**: there should exist sub-extremal states along a set of charge-space
   directions forming a basis, so extremal black holes of every charge (electric and
   magnetic) can decay — the seed of the later
   [[convex-hull-condition|convex hull condition]].
5. **Finiteness conjecture**: (III) would follow from "the number of exactly stable
   particles in a theory of quantum gravity in asymptotically flat space is finite" — with
   the immediate correction that stable BPS dyons of charge $(n,1)$ are infinite in number,
   so the right statement is finiteness *per direction in charge space*. Otherwise the
   tower of gravitationally bound (Kepler) states with monotonically decreasing $M/Q$ would
   be an infinite family of exactly stable states.
6. **$p$-form generalization**: for a $p$-form gauge field in $D$ dimensions, electric and
   magnetic branes with tensions $T_{\rm el} \lesssim (g^2/G_N)^{1/2}$,
   $T_{\rm mag} \lesssim (1/(g^2 G_N))^{1/2}$, where $[g] = {\rm mass}^{\,p+1-D/2}$; the
   inequalities are saturated (BPS bounds) when the objects carry central charges *only*,
   and strict for other types of charges. The $D=4$, $p=0$ (axion) case with
   $g \sim 1/F$ gives $S_{\rm inst} \lesssim M_{\rm Pl}/F$, subsuming
   Banks-Dine-Fox-Gorbatov; the $D=5$, $p=1$ case parametrically excludes
   [[extranatural-inflation]] (slow roll needs $g_5^2 M_5^3 R^2 \ll 1$, violating the
   bound).
7. **Heterotic check**: in $SO(32)$ heterotic on $T^6$ ($M_{\rm Pl}=1$, $\alpha'=4$), the
   perturbative spectrum $M^2 = \tfrac12 Q_R^2 + N_R = \tfrac12 Q_L^2 + N_L - 1$ always
   contains, for $Q_L^2 > Q_R^2$, a state with $M^2 = \tfrac12 Q_L^2 - 1$ — strictly below
   Sen's non-BPS extremal mass $M^2 = \tfrac12 Q_L^2$ (the $-1$ from the left-moving
   bosonic sector is crucial). BPS directions ($Q_R^2 > Q_L^2$, extremal mass
   $M^2 = \tfrac12 Q_R^2$) saturate the bound; S-duality extends the check to magnetic
   charges.
8. **CFT generalization**: for any *integral* $U(1)$ coming from the left-movers, in any
   perturbative heterotic compactification: bosonizing the level-$k$ current
   $J(z) \sim \sqrt{k}\,\partial\phi$, the operator
   $e^{i\sqrt{k}\phi}$ exists by completeness of the CFT spectrum (modular invariance) —
   equivalently spectral flow — giving an $N_L=0$ state with $M^2 = \tfrac12 Q_L^2 - 1$,
   sub-extremal.
9. **$F^4$-corrected extremality**: for
   $\mathcal L = -F_{\mu\nu}^2 + a(F^2)^2 + b(F\tilde F)^2 + \cdots$, to first order in
   $a,b$ ($M_{\rm Pl}=1$),
   $$M^2_{\rm extr} = (Q_e^2 + Q_m^2)
   - \frac{2a}{5}\frac{(Q_e^2 - Q_m^2)^2}{(Q_e^2+Q_m^2)^2}
   - \frac{32b}{5}\frac{Q_e^2 Q_m^2}{(Q_e^2+Q_m^2)^2}\,,$$
   so purely electric/magnetic black holes have $M^2_{\rm extr} = Q^2 - \tfrac{2a}{5}$:
   for the causality-preferred signs $a,b>0$, extremality is approached from *below* and
   slightly sub-extremal large black holes can themselves play the role of the required
   states ([[f4-corrected-extremality-bound]]). For $a=4b$ the formula has an $SO(2)$
   electric-magnetic symmetry. Effects of the remaining four-derivative operators
   ($R^2$, $RF^2$) deferred to later work.
10. **Phenomenology**: extrapolating the Standard Model with unification,
    $\Lambda \sim \sqrt{\alpha_{\rm GUT}/G_N} \sim 10^{17}$ GeV — new physics required
    below $M_{\rm Pl}$, near the heterotic string scale. A new gauge force with
    $g \sim 10^{-15}$ (sub-millimeter range) would require new physics at
    $g M_{\rm Pl} \sim$ TeV.

## Methods

- **Remnant argument** (presented under the section title "Loose conjectures"): with
  $g \sim 10^{-100}$, black holes of mass $\sim 10 M_{\rm Pl}$ carry any of
  $\sim 10^{100}$ charges; without light charged particles the charge cannot be radiated
  away as the hole Hawking-evaporates down to the Planck scale, leaving
  $10^{100}$ [[black-hole-remnant|Planck-scale remnants]] labeled by any of $\sim 10^{100}$
  charges, "suffering from the same problems that lead us to conclude quantum gravity
  shouldn't have global symmetries (see e.g. [[hep-th-9501106|Susskind 1995]])." The cited
  paper itself establishes only that an unbounded-degeneracy remnant species is
  thermodynamically inconsistent (it drives the renormalized Newton constant to zero via the
  Rindler thermal atmosphere); it does not itself discuss global or gauge symmetry — the link
  to symmetry-exclusion is this paper's own inference, by analogy of structure (an infinite
  tower of exactly-degenerate states), not a shared derivation.
- **Monopole-not-a-black-hole argument** (introduced as "Another heuristic argument"):
  $m_{\rm mon} \sim \Lambda/g^2$,
  $R_{\rm mon} \sim 1/\Lambda$; demanding the minimal monopole not be a black hole gives
  $\Lambda \lesssim g M_{\rm Pl}$.
- **Parametric checks across string constructions**: heterotic closed-string $U(1)$'s
  ($gM_{\rm Pl} \sim M_s$), KK and winding $U(1)$'s (T-dual), type I, D$p$-brane stacks,
  a lone D-brane with long-string $W$'s (marginal in $n=2$ large dimensions), D3-branes in
  $AdS_5\times X$ with a Planck brane. The many-$U(1)$ Higgsing loophole
  ($g_{\rm diag} \sim g/\sqrt N$) is closed because $N$ can't be made parametrically large
  without weakening gravity: the internal volume, and hence $M_{\rm Pl}^2$, grows as $N$,
  so $N$ cancels out of $g_{\rm diag} M_{\rm Pl}$.
- **Exact spectrum vs. extremality** in heterotic on $T^6$ (Narain lattice vs. Sen's black
  holes), generalized by the worldsheet spectral-flow/completeness argument.
- **Perturbed extremal solutions** to first order in the $F^4$ Wilson coefficients.

## Conventions

- Sec. 3 onward: $M_{\rm Pl}=1$, charge normalized so large extremal black holes have
  $M/Q = 1$ (extremality $M = Q\,M_{\rm Pl}$); no explicit $\sqrt2$/$4\pi$ normalization of
  $Q$ is fixed, and Sec. 2 statements carry only $\lesssim$/$\sim$.
- Heterotic section: $\alpha' = 4$; Narain charges $(Q_L|Q_R)$ on the even self-dual
  $\Gamma^{22,6}$ lattice, $Q_L^2 - Q_R^2 \in 2\mathbb Z$.
- The $F^4$ Lagrangian is normalized as $-F_{\mu\nu}^2$ (not $-\tfrac14 F^2$); the
  coefficients $\tfrac{2a}{5}$, $\tfrac{32b}{5}$ are tied to this normalization.
- BPS is a *limiting* case: "the BPS case is a limiting case of our bound, saturating it."

## Key equations

- Electric form: $m_{\rm el} \lesssim g\,M_{\rm Pl}$; magnetic form:
  $m_{\rm mag} \lesssim M_{\rm Pl}/g$; cutoff form: $\Lambda \lesssim g\,M_{\rm Pl}$
  (Sec. 2.1).
- $p$-form: $T_{\rm el} \lesssim (g^2/G_N)^{1/2}$,
  $T_{\rm mag} \lesssim (1/(g^2 G_N))^{1/2}$; axion case $S_{\rm inst} \lesssim M_{\rm Pl}/F$
  (Sec. 2.4).
- Sharp forms (Sec. 3): (I) $M_{q_{\rm min}}/q_{\rm min} \le 1$,
  (II) $M_{\rm min}/q_{M_{\rm min}} \le 1$, (III) $(M/q)_{\rm min} \le 1$.
- Heterotic spectrum vs. extremal masses (Sec. 4):
  $M^2 = \tfrac12 Q_R^2 + N_R = \tfrac12 Q_L^2 + N_L - 1$ vs. $M^2 = \tfrac12 Q_R^2$ (BPS)
  / $\tfrac12 Q_L^2$ (non-BPS).
- $F^4$-corrected extremality (Sec. 5, eq. `elmgbh`): see Main results 9.

## Open questions raised

- Is the strong form (II) true, or only (III)? If only (III), the minimal-$M/Q$ charge
  $Q_*$ is speculated to be "peaked for charges of order 1, but perhaps with sporadic
  exceptions" ([[wgc-strong-vs-weak-form]]).
- AdS version: "It would be interesting to investigate whether there is an analogous
  conjecture in Anti-de-Sitter spaces", as a statement about the dual CFT spectrum "that
  can perhaps be proved on general grounds" — the seed of the AdS-WGC line developed in
  [[nakayama-nomura-wgc-bound]] and [[modified-ads-wgc-3c-2a]].
- Effect of the remaining four-derivative operators ($R^2$, $RF^2$) on extremal masses
  (deferred to work then in progress).
- Is there a sharp obstacle to super-Planckian axion inflation (if (II)), or is it only
  non-generic (if — "as is more likely" — only (III))? Flagged as "two very different
  pictures"; a discovery of primordial gravitational waves might experimentally force
  trans-Planckian inflaton excursions, making the tension acute.
- No form of the conjecture is proven; Sec. 2 is titled "Loose conjectures" and the
  monopole argument is introduced as "Another heuristic argument".
