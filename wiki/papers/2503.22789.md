## TL;DR

Ordered phases (solids, ferromagnets, superfluids, topological order, superconductors) can persist to **arbitrarily high temperature** if ordering one set of degrees of freedom lets a second set — interacting bosons with an *unbounded* local Hilbert space — fluctuate far more, so the ordered configurations carry the *higher* entropy. The paper names this mechanism **[[entropic-order|entropic order]]**, gives explicit local classical and quantum lattice models plus large-$N$ QFTs that realize it, and shows it evades the standard no-go theorems on high-$T$ long-range order and entanglement (which all assume finite-dimensional on-site spaces). A toy model of high-$T_c$ superconductivity follows, in which the effective attraction $g_{\rm eff}(T)$ *grows* with temperature.

## Motivation

Conventional statistical mechanics reads $F = E - TS$: the high-temperature phase maximizes entropy and is therefore "disordered" — heating melts a solid, demagnetizes a magnet. For a lattice model with a *finite* unconstrained configuration space this is a theorem: at $\beta = 0$ the Gibbs measure is uniform independent of $H$, so no long-range order survives, and recent work shows finite-dimensional quantum Gibbs states even become fully separable above an $O(1)$ temperature (the [[heat-death-of-entanglement|"heat death of entanglement"]]). Yet several UV-complete QFTs are known to order at all $T$. The paper asks whether high-$T$ order is a field-theory artifact or can be realized by simple local lattice models, and what the unifying physical mechanism is.

## Historical context

- The physical prototype is the **[[pomeranchuk-effect|Pomeranchuk effect]]** in $^3$He (1950): solidifying the liquid releases nuclear-spin entropy, so the solid is the *higher*-temperature phase. Related intermediate-$T$ precedents: inverse melting, Rochelle-salt ordering, entropic effects in magic-angle graphene, and "order by disorder" (Villain).
- No-go theorems ruling out high-$T$ order/entanglement in finite-dimensional local models: Kliesch et al. (2014) and the [[heat-death-of-entanglement|separability theorem]] of Bakshi et al. (2024).
- High-$T$ (up to $T\to\infty$) order in QFT: the multicritical $O(N)$-type constructions of Chai, Komargodski et al. (2020) and the local unitary $d=2$ model of Hawashin–Komargodski (2024) — see [[high-temperature-order-in-qft]] — plus AdS/CFT versions by Buchel.
- Foundations reused: the Ising model, Kramers–Wannier duality and Kadanoff–Ceva disorder operators, Baxter's hardcore lattice gas, BCS theory, CSS/toric-code topological order and the 4d toric code (finite-$T$ topological order), Metropolis–Hastings Monte Carlo, Sherrington–Kirkpatrick spin glasses.

## Main results

1. **General entropic-order construction (transmutation bosons).** Take any classical $k$-local model $\bar H = \sum_S \bar H_S$ that is ordered at $\beta = 1$; append a boson $n_S \in \mathbb{Z}^+$ to each term and set $H = \sum_S e^{\bar H_S} n_S$. Summing over $n_S$ produces an effective model *pinned* at $\beta_{\rm eff} = 1$, so as $\beta \to 0$ the boson-dressed model reproduces the ordered correlators of $\bar H$. See [[transmutation-boson-mechanism]].
2. **Bosonic lattice-gas solid at high $T$.** The local model $H = U\sum_{u\sim v} n_u^2 n_v^2 + \sum_v n_v$ with $n_v \in \{0,1,2,\dots\}$ has a *low*-$T$ gas and a *high*-$T$ checkerboard solid that spontaneously breaks translation symmetry; Monte Carlo gives $\beta_c \approx 0.19$ at $U=1$ and the transition sits in the 2d Ising universality class.
3. **Exactly solvable high-$T$ ferromagnet.** Ising spins $s_v = \pm 1$ plus edge bosons $n_{uv}$ with $H = \sum_{u\sim v}(a - b\, s_u s_v)(n_{uv}+1)$ map to an Ising model whose effective coupling $B(\beta)$ *decreases* with $T$; if $a/b > 1+\sqrt2$ the ferromagnet survives to $T\to\infty$.
4. **Entropic topological order.** Dressing a CSS/stabilizer code (e.g. the 4d toric code) with commuting transmutation bosons gives a Gibbs state whose qubit reduced density matrix is [[entropic-topological-order|topologically ordered at arbitrarily high $T$]] — a counterexample to the finite-dimensional heat-death theorem.
5. **QFT realization.** The multicritical Lagrangian $\mathcal{L} = \tfrac12(\partial\psi)^2 + \tfrac12(\partial\vec\phi)^2 + \tfrac{\lambda}{4N}(\vec\phi^2 - \psi^2)^2$ in $2<d<3$ is entropically ordered: the large-$N$ free energy is minimized at $\bar\phi^2 = 0$, $\bar\psi^2 = c_3 T^{d-1} > 0$, breaking $\psi \to -\psi$ at all $T>0$, with the *entropy larger* in the ordered state.
6. **High-$T_c$ superconductivity.** Coupling BCS fermions to $N$ near-critical bosons makes the effective attraction $g_{\rm eff}(\sigma) = g/(1 - \tfrac12 g t \sigma)$ grow with $T$; the gap equation keeps a solution $\Delta^2 \sim t^{-1} T^{d-1} \neq 0$ for all $\beta\omega_* \gtrsim 1$ — an exponential enhancement of $T_c$. See [[high-temperature-entropic-superconductivity]].
7. **Complexity corollary.** A contrived boson coupling can send $\beta_{\rm eff} \to \infty$ as $\beta \to 0$; applied to an NP-hard Ising encoding (Sherrington–Kirkpatrick), almost all high-energy states become computationally hard to prepare.

## Methods

- **Entropy accounting.** Compare the partition-function weight of ordered vs disordered configurations: a single occupied site gives $\bar n_1 \sim T$, an adjacent pair gives $\bar n_2 \sim T^{1/4}$, so $\bar n_1^{L^2/2} \gg \bar n_2^{L^2}$ favors the checkerboard solid at high $T$.
- **[[transmutation-boson-mechanism|Transmutation bosons]].** Attach an unbounded bosonic mode to each interaction term; summing the geometric series over $n_S$ converts inverse temperature into an effective coupling, generating high-$T$ order from any ordered template.
- **Monte Carlo (Metropolis–Hastings).** Gibbs sampling of the infinite-local-space bosonic lattice gas; order parameter $\Delta$ and finite-size scaling with $\bar\beta = 1/8$, $\nu = 1$ confirm Ising universality.
- **Large-$N$ / Hubbard–Stratonovich thermal field theory.** Introduce $\sigma$ conjugate to $\vec\phi^2 - \psi^2$, integrate out $N-1$ scalars, solve the gap equation via the coincident-point propagator for $\mathcal F(\psi,\phi,T)$ and $\mathcal S(\psi,E)$.
- **BCS + Nambu-spinor path integral.** Hubbard–Stratonovich the attraction into a Cooper field $\Delta$, integrate out fermions ($\mathrm{tr}\log$), couple to the critical $O(N)$ sector, solve the coupled $\sigma,\phi,\Delta$ gap equations with Matsubara sums.
- **Stabilizer-code / quantum-channel arguments** to certify high-$T$ topological order, and a **Kramers–Wannier** analysis showing the Ising$^*$ high/low-$T$ exchange is *not* entropic order (its measure is not a Gibbs measure; the disorder operator is not a physical observable).

## Conventions

- Euclidean field theory throughout; $\beta = 1/T$ with $k_B = 1$, imaginary time $\tau \in [0,\beta)$. Spatial dimension $d$; the QFT is written in $d+1$ spacetime dimensions with $2<d<3$.
- Large-$N$: quartic coupling $\lambda/4N$; condensates $\psi = \sqrt N \bar\psi$, $\phi^a = (\sqrt N \bar\phi, 0,\dots)$; BCS interaction $-\tfrac{g}{N}\sum_{ab} c^\dagger_{\uparrow a} c^\dagger_{\downarrow a} c_{\downarrow b} c_{\uparrow b}$ over $N$ flavors. Matsubara: bosonic $\omega_n = 2n\pi T$, fermionic $\Omega_n = (2n+1)\pi T$.
- **Order** is defined by measure condensation: an ordered phase is a Gibbs measure concentrating on disjoint sets $A_1,\dots,A_R$ whose pairwise Hamming distance grows without bound, with $\sum_i \mathbb{P}_\beta(A_i) = 1 - o(1)$. **$k$-local** means $H = \sum_{|S|\le k} H_S$ with $\max_S|H_S| = O(1)$.
- The added superconductor coupling $t>0$ has mass dimension $d-3$ (dangerously irrelevant).

## Key equations

- Local high-$T$ solid: $H = U\sum_{u\sim v} n_u^2 n_v^2 + \sum_v n_v$, $\ n_v\in\{0,1,2,\dots\}$, with checkerboard order parameter $\Delta = \tfrac{1}{L^4}\big\langle (\sum_{x,y}(-1)^{x+y} n_{(x,y)})^2\big\rangle$; $\beta_c \approx 0.19$ at $U=1$.
- Solvable high-$T$ ferromagnet: $H = \sum_{u\sim v}(a - b\, s_u s_v)(n_{uv}+1)$, effective coupling $e^{2B} = e^{2\beta b}\tfrac{1 - e^{-\beta(a+b)}}{1 - e^{-\beta(a-b)}}$, $B(0) = \tfrac12\log\tfrac{a+b}{a-b}$; ordered for all $T$ if $a/b > 1+\sqrt2$.
- Transmutation mechanism: $H = \sum_S e^{\bar H_S} n_S \Rightarrow \lim_{\beta\to0}\langle A\rangle = \dfrac{\sum e^{-\bar H} A}{\sum e^{-\bar H}}$ (order of $\bar H$ at $\beta_{\rm eff}=1$).
- Large-$N$ free-energy density: $\dfrac{\mathcal F}{N} \approx -c_1 T^{d+1} + c_2 T^{-\frac{2}{d-2}}\big(c_3 T^{d-1} + \phi^2 - \psi^2\big)^{\frac{d}{d-2}} + \cdots$, minimized at $\phi^2 = 0$, $\psi^2 = c_3 T^{d-1}$.
- BCS gap equation: $\dfrac{1}{g_{\rm eff}} = \nu\displaystyle\int_0^{\beta\omega_*}\!dx\,\dfrac{\tanh(\tfrac12\sqrt{x^2 + (\beta\Delta)^2})}{\sqrt{x^2 + (\beta\Delta)^2}}$ (RHS $\to 0$ as $\beta\to0$: ordinary SC dies at high $T$).
- Critical-boson enhancement: $g_{\rm eff}(\sigma) = \dfrac{g}{1 - \tfrac12 g t\sigma}$, giving $\Delta^2 \sim t^{-1} T^{d-1}$ for all $T \lesssim \omega_*$.

## Open questions raised

- Does entropic order survive if the thermodynamic limit is taken *before* $\beta\to0$? Conjectured to hold when $\bar H$ is not infinitesimally close to a transition — see [[high-t-order-thermodynamic-limit]].
- The minimum $N$ (claimed $\gtrsim 3$, unreliable at leading order) at which the QFT entropic order actually onsets.
- Robustness of entropic-ordered superconductivity to dynamical $U(1)$ gauge fluctuations.
- Whether any real material (e.g. many-band systems with near-critical phonons) realizes entropic high-$T_c$ superconductivity, versus a mere zero-$T$ coupling enhancement.
