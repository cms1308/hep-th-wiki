## TL;DR

A thermodynamic argument against Planck-scale [[black-hole-remnant|remnants]] that store the
information falling into black holes. Unlike earlier objections it is not concerned with a
possible divergence in the rate of pair production: it shows that the presence of $e^{S_R}$
remnant species in the thermal atmosphere of Rindler space makes the exact equilibrium
entropy per unit horizon area diverge as $S_R\to\infty$, and by the
[[susskind-uglum-low-energy-theorem]] ($S/A = 1/4G$ with $G$ fully renormalized) this drives
the renormalized Newton constant to zero — the claimed "inconsistency of black hole
remnants."
Cited by [[hep-th-0601001|Arkani-Hamed-Motl-Nicolis-Vafa 2006]] as the reference for "the
same problems that lead us to conclude quantum gravity shouldn't have global symmetries,"
but the paper itself never discusses global or gauge symmetry — see Open questions raised.

## Motivation

The remnant resolution of the [[black-hole-information-paradox]] requires the number of
distinct remnant species to be infinite, so that remnants can keep track of all the possible
states that can form an arbitrarily large black hole — an infinite additional entropy $S_R$
above the Bekenstein–Hawking entropy, mass-independent "if it is not to modify black hole
thermodynamics." This apparently leads to an obvious thermodynamic catastrophe: any
thermodynamic system such as the sun would find it entropically infinitely favorable to turn
all its energy into zero-temperature remnants. Remnant theorists countered by proposing
mechanisms that enormously suppress the *rate* of remnant production, so that the
remnant-dominated true equilibrium is unattainable in any reasonable time; "argument has
gone back and forth over the consistency of such suppression mechanisms." This paper's aim
is an inconsistency argument that bypasses that dispute entirely: "it does not matter what
mechanisms may or may not exist to quickly bring these particles to equilibrium. The low
energy theorem relates $G$ to the exact equilibrium value of the entropy."

## Historical context

The paper is a short note; its six references map the mid-1990s remnant debate:

- Y. Aharonov, A. Casher, S. Nussinov, Phys. Lett. B191 (1987) 51 — the proposal that
  information falling into black holes ends up in a Planck-scale remnant.
- T. Banks, M. O'Laughlin, A. Strominger, Phys. Rev. D47 (1993) 4476 [hep-th/9211030] —
  remnant theories in which the object looks small from outside while its interior expands
  to enormous size ("cornucopions"), with proposed mechanisms suppressing pair production.
- S. Giddings, Phys. Rev. D49 (1994) 947 [hep-th/9304027] — the back-and-forth over the
  consistency of those suppression mechanisms.
- L. Susskind, J. Uglum, Phys. Rev. D50 (1994) 2700 [hep-th/9401070] — the
  [[susskind-uglum-low-energy-theorem|low-energy theorem]] the argument rests on: the
  Rindler entropy computed from the conical euclidean path integral depends only on the
  fully renormalized Newton constant, $S/A = 1/4G$.
- W. G. Unruh, Phys. Rev. D14 (1976) 870 — the Unruh thermal density matrix of Rindler
  space.
- K. S. Thorne, R. H. Price, D. A. MacDonald, *Black Holes: The Membrane Paradigm* (1986) —
  fiducial observers (FIDOs) at rest in accelerated Rindler coordinates, who see the thermal
  atmosphere.

Contextually (not cited in the paper) this sits within the post-1992 information-paradox
debate, in which [[leonard-susskind|Susskind]] was simultaneously developing black hole
complementarity; this note removes a competing resolution.

## Main results

1. **The low-energy theorem applies to the exact equilibrium state.** Rindler entropy from
   the conical path integral depends only on the fully renormalized $G$: $S/A = 1/4G$, and
   "radiative corrections to the coupling constant must be in one to one correspondence with
   contributions to the entropy in a consistent theory." The entropy in this relation is the
   full entropy of exact thermal equilibrium described by the Unruh density matrix — so
   production kinetics are irrelevant to the argument.
2. **Remnants overrun the Rindler thermal atmosphere.** A Planck-mass remnant species (any
   other mass can be substituted with no change in the argument) has equilibrium density
   $N_i \sim \exp(-2\pi\rho M_p)$ at proper distance $\rho$ from the horizon, where the
   local FIDO temperature is $T(\rho) = 1/2\pi\rho$. With $e^{S_R}$ distinct species the
   total density is $N_R \sim \exp[S_R - 2\pi\rho M_p]$, sensible only below close-packing
   (taken at the Planck density). For astronomically large $S_R$ it is entropically
   favorable for the density to saturate out to $\rho = S_R/M_p$: "when $S_R \to \infty$ the
   entire Rindler space fills up with remnants out to arbitrarily large distances, even to
   where the acceleration is negligible. This certainly looks like a theory which is out of
   control."
3. **The equilibrium entropy per unit area diverges — and is area-dependent, not additive.**
   Each close-packed remnant carries internal entropy $S_R$, so the entropy density in the
   filled region is $\sigma \sim S_R M_p^3$; integrating over $\rho$ up to $S_R/M_p$ gives
   $$\frac{S}{A} \sim S_R^2\, M_p^2.$$
   "While it was our intention to add a large additive constant to the entropy we instead
   find … an even bigger area dependent term."
4. **Ordinary black holes are safe — the contrast that isolates the pathology.** A black
   hole of mass $M$ with the usual $S_{B.H.} = 4\pi M^2 G$ materializes in the atmosphere at
   distance $\rho$ with probability $P = \exp[-2\pi\rho M + 4\pi M^2 G]$, valid for holes
   fitting well inside $\rho$ ($2MG < \rho$). Integrating over $M$ from $M_p$ up to
   $MG = \alpha\rho$ with $\alpha < 0.5$, the integral is dominated by the lower endpoint:
   $P = \exp(-2\pi\rho M_p)$. "Black holes are exponentially suppressed in the thermal
   atmosphere even though their entropy is enormous. Remnants, by contrast, would overrun
   the system" — because black hole entropy is tied to mass while $S_R$ is mass-independent
   and unbounded.
5. **Conclusion.** As $S_R \to \infty$ the entropy per unit area is driven to infinity "and
   with it the inverse of $G$ at asymptotic distances" — against the assumption that
   asymptotic gravity is governed by a finite Newton constant. "I can see no way to avoid
   this without giving up remnants."

## Methods

- **Conical/deficit-angle euclidean path integral** for Rindler entropy:
  $S = -\beta^2\partial_\beta[\log Z(\beta)/\beta]$ with deficit angle $2\pi - \beta$,
  evaluated via the effective gravitational action
  ([[susskind-uglum-low-energy-theorem|Susskind–Uglum]]), giving $S/A = 1/4G$.
- **Unruh thermal-atmosphere bookkeeping** in the membrane-paradigm FIDO picture: local
  proper temperature $T(\rho) = 1/2\pi\rho$, Boltzmann-suppressed densities
  $\sim e^{-2\pi\rho M_i}$ for species heavier than the local temperature, species
  degeneracy entering as a factor $e^{S_R}$.
- **Close-packing saturation estimate** ("for definiteness ... the Planck density") to
  determine how far out remnants fill the wedge; order-of-magnitude throughout ($\sim$, a
  $2\pi$ dropped between the saturation condition and $\rho = S_R/M_p$).
- **Endpoint-dominated integral** over black hole mass for the probability that an ordinary
  black hole materializes in the thermal atmosphere — the contrast case.

## Assumptions and qualifiers

Stated explicitly in the text:

- Remnants are "small objects of about the Planck size and Planck mass" *as seen from
  outside*; large-interior (cornucopion-type) remnants are explicitly accommodated — "For
  our purposes it is only important that when seen from outside, the remnant is small."
- The gravitational force at asymptotic distances is governed by a finite Newton constant
  $G$.
- $S_R$ is mass-independent ("if it is not to modify black hole thermodynamics") and the
  Planck-mass choice is inessential.
- Equilibrium only, no kinetics: the theorem relates $G$ to the exact equilibrium entropy,
  so rate-suppression mechanisms cannot evade the argument.
- Close-packing "for definiteness" at the Planck density.

Implicit, not flagged in the paper: the validity of the
[[susskind-uglum-low-energy-theorem]] itself when the species content is infinite, and the
treatment of remnants as ordinary particle species contributing to the thermal atmosphere
with degeneracy $e^{S_R}$. The paper does not analyze counterpositions in which remnant
internal states are not accessible as independent thermal species.

## Conventions

- Natural units; Planck mass written $M_p$; $S_{B.H.} = 4\pi M^2 G$ for a Schwarzschild
  black hole, consistent with $S = A/4G$.
- $G$ always means the fully renormalized (asymptotic/IR) Newton constant, distinguished
  from any bare value.

## Key equations

Numbered as printed in the source:

1. Rindler entropy from the conical partition function:
   $S = -\beta^2\,\partial_\beta\!\left[\frac{\log Z(\beta)}{\beta}\right]$ (eq. 1).
2. [[susskind-uglum-low-energy-theorem|Low-energy theorem]]: $\dfrac{S}{A} = \dfrac{1}{4G}$
   (eq. 2).
3. Unruh density matrix: $\rho_u = \dfrac{\exp(-2\pi H_r)}{Z}$, $H_r$ the Rindler
   Hamiltonian (eq. 3).
4. Local FIDO temperature: $T(\rho) = \dfrac{1}{2\pi\rho}$ (eq. 4).
5. Massive-species / remnant-species density: $N_i \sim \exp[-2\pi\rho M_i]$, remnants at
   $M_i = M_p$ (eqs. 5–6).
6. Total remnant density over $e^{S_R}$ species: $N_R \sim \exp[S_R - 2\pi\rho M_p]$
   (eq. 7).
7. Close-packing saturation radius: $S_R - 2\pi\rho M_p = 0 \Rightarrow \rho = S_R/M_p$
   (eqs. 8–9; the $2\pi$ dropped in eq. 9 as printed).
8. Entropy density and divergent entropy per area: $\sigma \sim S_R M_p^3$,
   $\ \dfrac{S}{A} \sim S_R^2 M_p^2$ (eqs. 10–11).
9. Probability for a black hole to materialize in the thermal atmosphere, and its endpoint
   dominance: $P = \exp[-2\pi\rho M + 4\pi M^2 G] \to \exp(-2\pi\rho M_p)$ (eqs. 12–13).

## Open questions raised

- Where the information goes if remnants are excluded is not addressed — the paper
  eliminates one branch of the [[black-hole-information-paradox]] without arguing here for
  its alternative.
- Whether any remnant theory can evade the argument is asserted, not proven ("I can see no
  way to avoid this without giving up remnants"); loopholes such as remnant internal states
  not behaving as $e^{S_R}$ independent thermal species, or the status of the
  [[susskind-uglum-low-energy-theorem]] when the species content is infinite, are not
  analyzed.
- The estimates are order-of-magnitude (Planck-density close-packing, dropped $2\pi$'s); no
  controlled calculation is given.
- **Global symmetries are never mentioned in this paper.** The word "symmetry" does not
  appear in the text; the argument is entirely Rindler thermodynamics plus the
  entropy–$G$-renormalization correspondence. When
  [[hep-th-0601001|Arkani-Hamed-Motl-Nicolis-Vafa 2006]] cite this paper (`\cite{remn}`) as
  the reason remnants "suffer from the same problems that lead us to conclude quantum
  gravity shouldn't have global symmetries," that connection is AMNV's own inference by
  analogy: an infinite tower of exactly degenerate, stable remnants labeled by an unbounded
  charge is the structure an exact global symmetry would protect, and this paper is the
  standard reference that such a structure is thermodynamically inconsistent — but the
  symmetry side of the link is not established by this source.
  [[no-global-symmetries-in-quantum-gravity]] (still to be written) needs an additional
  primary source tying black-hole physics directly to symmetry violation (e.g.
  wormhole-induced global-charge non-conservation, Giddings–Strominger 1988).
