## TL;DR

Lecture notes developing a **field-theoretic theory of neural networks**, organized around three
pillars: **expressivity** (what a network can represent), **statistics** (the ensemble of networks
at random initialization), and **dynamics** (evolution under gradient descent). The central
dictionary is that an ensemble of randomly initialized networks *is* a Euclidean field theory whose
$n$-point correlators $G^{(n)}(x_1,\dots,x_n)=\langle\phi(x_1)\cdots\phi(x_n)\rangle$ can be computed
in **parameter space** (integral over the parameter density $P(\theta)$) or in **function space**
(path integral $\int\mathcal D\phi\,e^{-S[\phi]}$). Infinite-width networks are generalized free
fields (the [[neural-network-gaussian-process|NNGP correspondence]]); $1/N$ and broken statistical
independence turn on interactions; gradient descent is governed by the
[[neural-tangent-kernel]]. The logic is finally inverted into an
[[neural-network-field-theory|NN-FT/NN-QFT correspondence]] that *defines* field theories (including
$\phi^4$) from network ensembles.

## Motivation

Machine-learning experiment has outrun ML theory; the analogy drawn is 1960s particle physics —
awash in hadrons, awaiting an organizing principle. The claim is that a theoretical physicist's
toolkit (field theory, ensemble statistics, symmetry, large-$N$ expansion, gradient dynamics) is the
natural language for an $O(1)$, non-rigorous mechanistic understanding of neural networks. The key
reframing: what a network "predicts" is not the single function $\phi_\theta(x)$ but statistical
expectations over the random initialization $\theta\sim P(\theta)$ and over training trajectories
$\theta(t)$ — i.e. **correlation functions**. In that sense machine learning is field theory.

## Historical context

- **Universal approximation** — Cybenko (1989, sigmoid single-hidden-layer); Hornik (1991,
  generalization).
- **Kolmogorov–Arnold representation** — Kolmogorov & Arnold (1957); revived recently as
  [[kolmogorov-arnold-network|KANs]] (Liu et al. 2024).
- **NN/Gaussian-process correspondence** — Neal (1996, infinite-width single-layer nets are Gaussian
  processes); Williams (1997, explicit kernel). Extended to deep/convolutional/attention nets
  (Yang 2019 and others).
- **Non-Gaussianities / NN effective field theory** — Yaida (2020); Roberts–Yaida–Hanin *Principles
  of Deep Learning Theory* (2022); Demirtas–Halverson–Maiti–Schwartz–Stoner (2023).
- **Neural tangent kernel** — Jacot–Gabriel–Hongler (2018); Lee et al. (2019, wide-network
  linearization and exact training).
- **Feature learning / $\mu P$** — Yang (2020); Bordelon–Pehlevan (2022, dynamical mean-field
  theory); Pehlevan–Bordelon lecture notes (2024) followed in the dynamics section.
- **NN-FT / NN-QFT** — Halverson–Maiti–Stoner (2021); Halverson (2021), using the
  Osterwalder–Schrader axioms (1973) as the continuation criterion.

## Main results

Organized by the lectures' three pillars.

**Expressivity.**
- [[universal-approximation-theorem|Universal approximation]] (Cybenko): a single-hidden-layer
  feedforward net can $\epsilon$-approximate any continuous function on a compact set — an existence
  statement, silent on width and on trainability.
- Kolmogorov–Arnold representation and the [[kolmogorov-arnold-network|KAN]] architecture, with
  learned activations on edges that can be read off as interpretable symbolic formulas.

**Statistics.**
- [[nngp-correspondence|NNGP correspondence]]: a width-$N$ feedforward net is a sum of $N$ i.i.d.
  neuron functions, so by a functional CLT the $N\to\infty$ ensemble is a Gaussian process — a
  generalized free field with quadratic action and kernel $G^{(2)}$.
- Finite $N$ or broken independence violate the CLT and give a nonzero connected 4-point function
  at $O(1/N)$ — an interaction. Worked models: **Gauss-net**, **Cos-net** (Euclidean-invariant at
  all $N$), and **Scalar-net**, which reproduces the free scalar propagator $1/(p^2+m^2)$.
- Global symmetries of an ensemble follow from an invariant $P(\theta)$ (e.g. even $P(w)$ gives a
  $\mathbb Z_2$ $\phi\to-\phi$ symmetry, killing odd correlators); equivariant nets are the special
  case that converts input transformations into output transformations.

**Dynamics.**
- The [[neural-tangent-kernel|NTK]] controls gradient-descent evolution; at $N\to\infty$ in the
  "lazy" regime it freezes at its deterministic initialization value.
- Frozen NTK + MSE loss makes training exactly solvable — the trained network equals **kernel
  regression**, with exact memorization on training points. Drawback: no
  [[feature-learning|feature learning]] (hidden layers do not move).
- [[maximal-update-parameterization|Maximal update parameterization ($\mu P$)]]: the unique
  $N$-scaling of weights and learning rate that keeps pre-activations, predictions, and features all
  $O(1)$ and evolving in $O(1)$ time — the parameterization in which wide networks *do* learn
  features.

**Synthesis — NN-FT.**
- [[neural-network-field-theory|NN-FT / NN-QFT correspondence]]: the pair $(\phi_\theta,P(\theta))$
  *is* a Euclidean field theory; if it also satisfies the Osterwalder–Schrader axioms it defines a
  quantum field theory. Inserting a local potential $e^{\int V(\phi_\theta)}$ into a free (NNGP)
  ensemble deforms $P(\theta)$, breaks independence, and builds an interacting theory — e.g.
  **$\phi^4$** from the Scalar-net.

## Methods

- **Parameter-space vs function-space duality.** Compute correlators either as
  $Z[J]=\int d\theta\,P(\theta)\,e^{\int J\phi_\theta}$ (always available from network data) or as
  $Z[J]=\int\mathcal D\phi\,e^{-S[\phi]+\int J\phi}$; two dual descriptions of one theory.
- **Functional CLT.** A width-$N$ sum of i.i.d. neurons has cumulants
  $\kappa_r^\phi=\kappa_r^{X}/N^{r/2-1}$, so all $\kappa_{r>2}\to0$ as $N\to\infty$: connected
  correlators vanish and the theory is free. $1/N$ and independence-breaking are the two knobs for
  interactions.
- **Williams's parameter-space computation.** Evaluate $\langle w\cdots w\rangle\langle\sigma\cdots
  \sigma\rangle$ with i.i.d. contractions; the residual single-neuron expectation is done
  analytically or by Monte Carlo over $w^{(0)}\sim P(w^{(0)})$, as in lattice field theory.
- **Action from kernel inversion.** For a zero-mean NNGP,
  $S[\phi]=\int\phi(x)\,G^{(2)}(x,y)^{-1}\phi(y)$ with
  $\int G^{(2)-1}(x,y)\,G^{(2)}(y,z)=\delta^{(d)}(x-z)$.
- **NTK linearization.** Replace the parameter-summed NTK by its expectation $\bar\Theta$ at
  $N\to\infty$ and solve the resulting linear ODE in function space.
- **$N$-scaling analysis.** Carry undetermined scaling exponents through pre-activation covariances,
  feature kernels, and gradient chain rules, then solve the exponent constraints — yielding $\mu P$.
- **Osterwalder–Schrader engineering.** Build Euclidean-invariant input layers, then check
  reflection positivity / cluster decomposition and add potentials.

## Conventions

Euclidean field theory throughout, in $d$-dimensional input ("spacetime") $x$. Networks map
$\phi_\theta:\mathbb R^d\to\mathbb R$; ensemble expectations $\langle\cdot\rangle$ are over
$\theta\sim P(\theta)$ at initialization (statistics) or over the trained trajectory (dynamics).
$N$ denotes the width (fully-connected), number of channels (CNN), or number of heads (attention).
$\mu_k$ are moments of the last-layer weight distribution. No overlap with the vault's holography
conventions — this is a self-contained ML area.

## Key equations

- Correlators as ML observables: $G^{(1)}(x)=\langle\phi(x)\rangle$ (mean prediction),
  $G^{(2)}(x,y)=\langle\phi(x)\phi(y)\rangle$.
- UAT architecture:
  $\phi(x)=\sum_{i}\sum_{j} w_i^{(1)}\,\sigma\!\big(w^{(0)}_{ij}x_j+b^{(0)}_i\big)+b^{(1)}$.
- Kolmogorov–Arnold representation:
  $f(x_1,\dots,x_n)=\sum_{q=0}^{2n}\Phi_q\!\Big(\sum_{p=1}^{n}\phi_{q,p}(x_p)\Big)$.
- NNGP kernel: $G^{(2)}(x,y)=\mu_2\,\big\langle\sigma(w^{(0)}_{ij}x_j)\,\sigma(w^{(0)}_{il}y_l)
  \big\rangle$ (no sum on $i$), free action $S[\phi]=\int\phi\,G^{(2)-1}\phi$.
- Connected 4-point (interaction):
  $G^{(4)}_c=\tfrac1N\big(\mu_4\langle\varphi\varphi\varphi\varphi\rangle
  -\mu_2^2(\langle\varphi\varphi\rangle\langle\varphi\varphi\rangle+\text{perms})\big)$ (no sum on $i$).
- NTK and its flow: $\Theta(x,x')=\partial_{\theta_i}\phi(x)\,\partial_{\theta_i}\phi(x')$,
  $\dot\phi(x)=\dfrac{\eta}{|\mathcal D|}\sum_\alpha\Delta(x_\alpha)\,\Theta(x,x_\alpha)$.
- Kernel-regression solution:
  $\phi_\infty(x)=\phi_0(x)+\bar\Theta(x,x_\alpha)\,\bar\Theta_{\alpha\beta}^{-1}
  \big(y_\beta-\phi_0(x_\beta)\big)$.
- $\mu P$ learning-rate scaling $\eta=\eta_0\gamma_0^2 N^{2d-c}$; feature-learning solution
  $d=\tfrac12,\ c=1$ (the lazy/NTK regime is $d=0$).
- NN-QFT deformation:
  $Z[J]=\int d\theta\,P(\theta)\,e^{\int V(\phi_\theta)}e^{\int J\phi_\theta}
  =\int d\theta\,\tilde P(\theta)\,e^{\int J\phi_\theta}$ — interactions = broken independence in
  $\tilde P(\theta)$.

## Open questions raised

- Systematic engineering of NN-FTs $(\phi_\theta,P(\theta))$ satisfying **reflection positivity**
  and **cluster decomposition**.
- Building an NN-FT with a *prescribed* action; realizing **locality** (available at infinite $N$ —
  is there an obstruction at finite $N$?).
- Mining classical approximation theory for new architectures, the way KART motivated KANs.
- A unifying principle combining statistics, dynamics, and architecture into a theory of optimal
  learning (why do Transformers work?).
- Whether NN-FTs can motivate genuinely new physical theories or tools (CFT and Grassmann-field
  NN-FT flagged as forthcoming).
- Whether a deeper theory of (sparse) networks lets small models match giant LLMs.
