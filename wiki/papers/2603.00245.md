## TL;DR

For a phase transition out of a $2+1$-dimensional topological phase (a TQFT $\mathcal T$) triggered by a single Abelian anyon $a$ becoming light, the paper writes down the most general relativistic continuum theory: couple $\mathcal T$ to one complex scalar $\Phi$ that creates $a$. For fixed $\mathcal T$ and $a$ this [[chern-simons-matter-transition-theory|transition theory]] depends on only **one extra integer** $p$ — the anomaly of the $\mathbb Z_n^{(1)}$ one-form symmetry generated by $a$, kept as an integer rather than reduced mod $2n$. For $p\neq0$ the phase after the transition is a TQFT $\mathcal T'$ related to $\mathcal T$ by an [[abelian-hierarchy-construction|Abelian hierarchy construction]]; for $p=0$ it is gapless, and a specific deformation turns the transition into gauging an anomaly-free one-form symmetry, $\mathcal T\to\mathcal T/\mathbb Z_n$. The authors argue "anyon condensation" is a misnomer — nothing condenses, anyon worldlines proliferate — and rename these [[proliferation-transition|proliferation transitions]].

## Motivation

Transitions out of a gapped topological phase in $2+1$d are ubiquitous in the fractional quantum Hall and spin-liquid literature, usually described loosely as "anyon condensation." What was missing was a systematic **continuum field-theory** account, valid independently of UV microscopics, that describes both gapped phases *and* the critical point in one framework. The paper supplies an intermediate-energy [[chern-simons-matter-transition-theory|transition field theory]] (its Figure 1 organizes the "three scales": the deep-IR phases $\mathcal T$/$\mathcal T'$, the transition, and the UV) built from the minimal ingredient — a single complex scalar $\Phi$ creating the light Abelian anyon $a$.

A second motivation is conceptual: the standard "condensation" language is misleading. No local order parameter acquires a vev generically, no global symmetry is spontaneously broken, and no particle density becomes macroscopic. What happens is that the worldlines of the anyon $a$ proliferate in the path integral — hence [[proliferation-transition|proliferation transition]].

## Historical context

- Chern–Simons description of $2+1$d TQFT and its line operators / anyons (Witten 1988; Elitzur–Moore–Schwimmer–Seiberg 1989; Moore–Seiberg 1989; Gukov–Witten 2008).
- The [[abelian-hierarchy-construction|Abelian hierarchy]] of fractional quantum Hall states (Haldane 1983; Halperin 1984), its $K$-matrix / TQFT formulations (Blok–Wen 1990; Read 1990; Wen–Zee 1992), its recasting as a TQFT-to-TQFT map (Lan–Wang–Wen 2017; Lan 2019), and its recognition as a stack-and-condense (gauging/enrichment) sequence (Cheng–Seifnashri et al. 2022).
- [[one-form-global-symmetry|Generalized (one-form) global symmetries]] and their gauging (Gaiotto–Kapustin–Seiberg–Willett 2014); minimal TQFTs $\mathcal A^{n,p}$ and the anomaly of $\mathbb Z_n^{(1)}$ (Moore–Seiberg 1988; Hsin–Lam–Seiberg 2018).
- "Anyon condensation" as a categorical operation (Bais–Slingerland 2008; Moore–Seiberg 1989) — the language this paper reframes; Dijkgraaf–Witten twists (1990).
- Chern–Simons–matter descriptions of condensed-matter transitions (Kivelson et al. 1992; Wen 1993; Chen et al. 1993; Barkeshli 2012; Goldman et al. 2019–2020).
- $2+1$d particle/vortex and boson/fermion dualities (Karch–Tong 2016; Seiberg–Senthil–Wang–Witten 2016) and $SL(2,\mathbb Z)$ $S,T$ operations (Witten 2003).

The $p=0$ "gauging as a Higgs transition" construction is generalized here from an unpublished recipe (attributed in the acknowledgements to work with S. Todadri) to all $p$.

## Main results

**1. Two-TQFT map controlled by an integer $p$.** For a bosonic TQFT $\mathcal T$ with Abelian anyon $a$ of order $n$ ($a^n=\mathbf 1$) and spin
$$h(a)=\frac{p}{2n}\bmod 1,\qquad pn\in 2\mathbb Z\qquad(\text{Eq. 2.1}),$$
the phase after the transition is
$$\mathcal T'=\frac{\mathcal T\boxtimes \mathrm U(1)_{-pn}}{\mathbb Z_n}=\frac{\mathcal T\boxtimes\mathrm U(1)_{-pn}}{(a,p)},\qquad \mathcal L_{\mathrm U(1)_{-np}}=-\frac{np}{4\pi}\,b\,db\qquad(\text{Eq. 2.16, 2.20}).$$
For fixed $\mathcal T$ and $a$ this depends only on the integer $p$ — crucially not only on $p\bmod 2n$. Equivalently, with the $\mathbb Z_n$-twisted Dijkgraaf–Witten theory $\mathcal T_{n,p}$ ($\mathcal L=-\frac{np}{4\pi}b\,db+\frac n{2\pi}b\,dc$, Eq. 2.5), $\mathcal T\cong\frac{\mathcal T\boxtimes\mathcal T_{n,p}}{(a,p,-1)}$ (Eq. 2.9).

**2. The transition field theory is essentially unique.** With the duality relation $\mathcal L_{\mathcal T'}+\frac1{2\pi}\mathfrak b\,dc\leftrightarrow\mathcal L_{\mathcal T}$ (Eq. 2.28), the most general Lorentz-invariant transition theory with one complex scalar is
$$\mathcal L=\mathcal L_{\mathcal T'}+\frac1{2\pi}\mathfrak b\,dc+|D_c\Phi|^2-\mu^2|\Phi|^2+\cdots,\qquad D_c\Phi=(d-ic)\Phi\qquad(\text{Eq. 3.1}),$$
with $\Phi$ creating the anyon $a\simeq e^{i\int c}$.

**3. Phases for $p\neq0$.** Tuning $\mu^2$: for $\mu^2>0$ the IR is $\mathcal T$; for $\mu^2<0$, $\Phi$ Higgses $c$ and the IR is $\mathcal T'$. Each TQFT is the hierarchy construction of the other (inverse map with $\tilde a=(\mathbf 1,n)$, $\tilde n=p$, $\tilde p=-n$).

**4. Emergent magnetic $\mathrm U(1)_{\mathcal B}$.** The transition theory has one continuous zero-form current,
$$J_{\mathcal B}=\frac{\ell}{n}\,\mathscr J(\Phi)=\frac{\ell}{2\pi n}\,d\mathfrak b,\qquad \ell\mid L=\gcd(n,p)\qquad(\text{Eq. 3.15}),$$
a winding/magnetic symmetry; a $\mathbb Z_\ell^{\mathcal B}$ subgroup acts only on line operators. The minimal-charge local operator is $\mathcal O=\Phi^n\,\mathcal M_{\mathfrak b}^n\,\mathcal M_c^{p}$ with $Q_{\mathcal B}(\mathcal O)=\ell$ (Eq. 3.19).

**5. Hall-conductivity jump.** Though $\mathrm U(1)_{\mathcal B}$ is unbroken in both phases it participates in the transition:
$$\sigma_H^{\mathcal B}(\mu^2>0)-\sigma_H^{\mathcal B}(\mu^2<0)=\frac{\ell^2}{np}\qquad(\text{Eq. 3.24}).$$

**6. $p=0$ = gauging a one-form symmetry.** Here $\mathcal T'$ is not a TQFT; the $\mu^2<0$ phase is gapless with a Goldstone of spontaneously broken $\mathrm U(1)_{\mathcal B}$. Adding $\mathcal O=\Phi^n$ (Eq. 4.5) gaps it and gives $\mathcal T\to\mathcal T/\mathbb Z_n=\mathcal T/(a)$ — a Higgs transition that implements [[gauging-one-form-symmetry|gauging]] the anomaly-free $\mathbb Z_n^{(1)}$ generated by $a$. The theory splits (up to a $\mathbb Z_n$ gauge field) into $\mathcal T/\mathbb Z_n$ times an XY model for $\Phi$; the transition is BKT-like (winding symmetry emergent in one phase, explicitly broken in the other).

**7. Particle/vortex dual.** The dual description $\mathcal L_{\mathcal T'}+|D_{\mathfrak b}\tilde\Phi|^2+\dots$ with $\tilde\mu^2=-\mu^2$ (Eq. 5.6) realizes the inverse map: after the transition the light mode is $\tilde\Phi$ creating $\tilde a=(\mathbf 1,n)\in\mathcal T'$.

**8. Enrichment by $\mathrm U(1)_A$ and an incompatibility.** Enriching $\mathcal T$ via a vison $v$ gives $Q_A(a)=\langle v,a\rangle=\frac{n_v}{n}\bmod 1$; the transition coupling depends on the integer $n_v$, with $Q_A(\mathcal O)=n_v$ and $\sigma_H^A(\mu^2<0)=\sigma_H^A(\mu^2>0)-\frac{n_v^2}{pn}$ (Eq. 6.16). When $n_v\neq0\bmod n$ (fractional $\mathrm U(1)_A$ charge of $a$), $\mathrm U(1)_A$ cannot act as pure enrichment; in particular for $p=0$ the gauging transition is incompatible with the enrichment and the system is forced into a gapless phase with spontaneously broken $\mathrm U(1)_A$.

**9. Examples.** General Abelian $\mathcal T$ ($K$-matrix), with $\mathcal T'$ carrying $K'=\begin{pmatrix}0&-q^{\mathrm T}\\-q&K\end{pmatrix}$, $\det K'=-\frac pn\det K$ (Eq. 8.10); Jain-series transitions $\mathcal J_{r,s}\to\mathcal J_{r\pm1,s}$; $\mathrm U(1)_8\to\mathbb Z_4$; and non-Abelian $\mathrm{SU}(2)_k\to\mathrm{SO}(3)_{4N}$ for $p=0$, where $\ell=L=\gcd(2,k)$ can equal $2$ (unlike the Abelian case where $\ell=1$).

## Methods

- **[[gauging-one-form-symmetry|TQFT quotient / one-form gauging]]:** anyons $(a,p)$ (or $(a,p,-1)$) gauged by summing over line insertions via the three-step Moore–Seiberg procedure (project onto trivially-braiding lines, identify orbits, add twisted descendants), reviewed in Appendix A.
- **Twisted gauge fields:** gauging $\mathbb Z_n^{(1)}$ turns the ordinary $\mathrm U(1)$ field $b$ into a twisted $\check b$ with fractional fluxes $\int d\check b\in\frac{2\pi}{n}\mathbb Z$; the standard partner $\mathfrak b=n\check b$ carries the gauge-invariant data, $\mathbf g^{(2)}=[d\mathfrak b/2\pi]_n$ (Eqs. 2.11–2.15).
- **Flux quantization on $\mathcal C_2$:** demanding an empty Hilbert space on $S^2$ with one $a$-defect fixes $\ell\mid L$ (Eq. 3.13); higher genus gives weaker constraints.
- **Symmetry fractionalization / enrichment:** a vison $v$ assigns $Q_A(s)=\langle v,s\rangle$ and a response $\sigma_H^A=2h(v)\bmod 2$; the $0$-form group is centrally extended, $G^{(0)}\to\tilde G^{(0)}$ (Appendix A).
- **Particle/vortex duality** relating $\Phi\leftrightarrow\tilde\Phi$; **$K$-matrix Chern–Simons** for the Abelian examples; $\mathrm U(2)/\mathrm{SO}(3)$ decomposition for the $\mathrm{SU}(2)_k$ example.

## Conventions

Relativistic Lorentz-invariant $2+1$d; signature not stated explicitly, forms with suppressed wedge products, "coefficients of order one suppressed." Currents are forms $J_B=-\delta\mathcal L/\delta B$; a Chern–Simons term $\frac k{2\pi}B\,dC$ contributes $J_B=-\frac k{2\pi}dC$. Uppercase = background, lowercase = dynamical gauge fields; boldface ($\mathbf c,\mathbf g$) = discrete cocycle gauge fields. The three related $\mathrm U(1)$ fields are $b$ (standard) $\to\check b$ (twisted, fractional flux) $\to\mathfrak b=n\check b$ (new standard).

The **load-bearing nonstandard choice**: the anomaly of $\mathbb Z_n^{(1)}$ is $h(a)=\frac{p}{2n}\bmod 1$ with $pn\in2\mathbb Z$; as a bare TQFT label $p\sim p+2n$, but the transition theory (and $L=\gcd(n,p)$) depends on the integer lift $p\in\mathbb Z$. Hall conductivity uses the response $-\frac{\sigma_H}{4\pi}\mathcal B\,d\mathcal B$, defined mod even integers (bosonic SPT stacking). $\mathrm{SO}(3)_k\equiv\mathrm{SU}(2)_k/\mathbb Z_2$.

## Key equations

- $h(a)=\frac{p}{2n}\bmod 1$, $pn\in2\mathbb Z$ (Eq. 2.1) — spin/anomaly of the $\mathbb Z_n^{(1)}$ generator; defines the integer $p$.
- $\mathcal T'=\dfrac{\mathcal T\boxtimes\mathrm U(1)_{-pn}}{(a,p)}$ (Eq. 2.16) — the far-side TQFT for $p\neq0$.
- $\mathcal L=\mathcal L_{\mathcal T'}+\frac1{2\pi}\mathfrak b\,dc+|D_c\Phi|^2-\mu^2|\Phi|^2+\cdots$ (Eq. 3.1) — the unique transition field theory.
- $-\frac{np}{4\pi}\check b\,d\check b+\frac n{2\pi}\check b\,dc\to\frac n{4\pi p}c\,dc$ (Eqs. 3.3–3.4) — local dynamics depends only on the rational $n/p$.
- $J_{\mathcal B}=\frac\ell n\mathscr J(\Phi)=\frac\ell{2\pi n}d\mathfrak b$, $\ell\mid L$ (Eq. 3.15) — quantized emergent $\mathrm U(1)_{\mathcal B}$ current.
- $\mathcal O=\Phi^n\mathcal M_{\mathfrak b}^n\mathcal M_c^{p}$, $Q_{\mathcal B}=\ell$ (Eq. 3.19) — minimal gauge-invariant local operator.
- $\sigma_H^{\mathcal B}(\mu^2>0)-\sigma_H^{\mathcal B}(\mu^2<0)=\frac{\ell^2}{np}$ (Eq. 3.24).
- $p=0$: $\mathcal T\to\mathcal T/\mathbb Z_n$ on adding $\mathcal O=\Phi^n$ (Sec. 4) — transition = gauging.
- $K'=\begin{pmatrix}0&-q^{\mathrm T}\\-q&K\end{pmatrix}$, $\det K'=-\frac pn\det K$ (Eq. 8.10) — Abelian $\mathcal T'$ $K$-matrix.
- $\sigma_H^A(\mu^2<0)=\sigma_H^A(\mu^2>0)-\frac{n_v^2}{pn}$ (Eq. 6.16) — $\mathrm U(1)_A$ Hall-conductivity change.

## Open questions raised

- Extension to **fermionic** and **electronic** (spin$^c$, $\mathrm U(1)_A$-coupled) theories via boson/fermion dualities.
- Transitions with $\Phi$ in **higher-dimensional representations**, expected to gauge **non-invertible one-form symmetries** (e.g. $\mathrm{SU}(2)_{10}\to\mathrm{Spin}(5)_1$) — see [[gauging-noninvertible-one-form-symmetry-transitions]].
- **Multicritical** points where several mutually non-local anyons go light together.
- Transitions triggered by **non-Abelian anyons** (non-invertible symmetry).
- The **soft symmetries** (acting only on line junctions) inside $\mathrm U(1)_{\mathcal B}$.
- The **$c=1$ chiral CFT interface** created by a spatially varying $\mu^2(x)$.
