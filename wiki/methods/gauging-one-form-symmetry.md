## What it computes

Given a $2+1$-dimensional TQFT $\mathcal T$ with an anomaly-free [[one-form-global-symmetry|$\mathbb Z_n^{(1)}$ one-form symmetry]] generated by an Abelian anyon $a$, gauging it produces the quotient TQFT $\mathcal T/\mathbb Z_n=\mathcal T/(a)$ — the field-theoretic content of [[anyon-condensation|condensing $a$]].

## Prerequisites

- A [[chern-simons-theory|Chern–Simons TQFT]] and its anyon (line-operator) content.
- An Abelian anyon $a$ of order $n$ generating a $\mathbb Z_n^{(1)}$ symmetry with vanishing anomaly, $h(a)=0\bmod 1$ (the $p=0$ case).

## How it works

The three-step Moore–Seiberg procedure (reviewed in Appendix A of [[2603.00245|Cheng–Seiberg 2026]]):

1. **Project** onto lines that braid trivially with $a$ — only these survive gauging.
2. **Identify** lines that differ by fusion with $a$ (they lie in the same orbit).
3. **Add twisted descendants** — new line operators from the twisted sectors of the gauged symmetry.

Concretely one sums over insertions of the anyon line $a$. After gauging, an ordinary $\mathrm U(1)$ dynamical field $b$ becomes a **twisted** field $\check b$ with fractional fluxes $\int d\check b\in\frac{2\pi}{n}\mathbb Z$; the gauge-invariant data is carried by the standardly normalized $\mathfrak b=n\check b$, with two-form background $\mathbf g^{(2)}=[d\mathfrak b/2\pi]_n$.

In the [[proliferation-transition|proliferation-transition]] realization, gauging is dynamical: it is a Higgs transition in which the scalar $\Phi$ (creating $a$) condenses after adding the operator $\mathcal O=\Phi^n$, producing $\mathcal T/\mathbb Z_n$ — see [[p-zero-transition-implements-gauging]].

## Regime of validity

Requires the one-form symmetry to be **anomaly-free** ($p=0\bmod 2n$); a nonzero anomaly obstructs gauging while preserving Lorentz invariance, and the transition instead lands on a TQFT $\mathcal T'$ via the [[abelian-hierarchy-construction]]. For Abelian $a$ the gauged symmetry is invertible; higher-dimensional $\Phi$ representations or non-Abelian anyons require gauging **non-invertible** one-form symmetries — see [[gauging-noninvertible-one-form-symmetry-transitions]].

## Canonical applications

- $\mathrm{SU}(2)_k\to\mathrm{SO}(3)_{4N}$ ($p=0$) and $\mathrm U(1)_8\to\mathbb Z_4$ in [[2603.00245|Cheng–Seiberg 2026]].
- Condensing bosons in fractional quantum Hall and spin-liquid topological orders (general [[topological-phase-transitions-2plus1d]] setting).
