## Definition

The **Pomeranchuk effect** (Pomeranchuk 1950) is the phenomenon in $^3$He whereby *solidifying* the liquid is favored by *raising* the temperature over a range of the phase diagram: along the melting curve, compressing liquid $^3$He at low temperature makes it freeze. The driving force is entropy — the solid holds more of it than the liquid — so it is the physical prototype of [[entropic-order|entropic order]].

## Motivation

In solid $^3$He the nuclear spins are nearly free and disordered above the nuclear-ordering scale (a few mK), contributing $\sim \log 2$ of entropy per atom. In the liquid the same atoms are degenerate fermions whose spin entropy is quenched by Fermi statistics (entropy $\sim T/T_F$, small). Over the intermediate temperature window the *solid* therefore has the larger entropy, so via $F = E - TS$ solidification is entropically favored as temperature rises — freezing on heating, the reverse of ordinary melting.

## Key formulas

- Clausius–Clapeyron along the melting curve: $\dfrac{dP}{dT} = \dfrac{S_{\rm solid} - S_{\rm liquid}}{V_{\rm solid} - V_{\rm liquid}}$. Because $S_{\rm solid} > S_{\rm liquid}$ while $V_{\rm solid} < V_{\rm liquid}$, the melting curve has *negative* slope $dP/dT < 0$ — the signature of the effect.

## Relations

- The intermediate-temperature ancestor of the [[entropic-order]] mechanism: order (the solid) is stabilized by the entropy of a second set of degrees of freedom (the nuclear spins), exactly as transmutation bosons stabilize order in the lattice models.
- Same family as order-by-disorder and inverse melting.
