## Definition

The **2-Witt algebra** $\mathfrak{witt}(2)$ is the holomorphic-twist enhancement of spacetime
symmetry: the (derived) algebra of holomorphic vector fields on punctured $\mathbb C^2$,
$$\mathfrak{witt}(2)\simeq\Omega^{0,\bullet}(\mathbb C^2\setminus\{0\},\,T^{1,0}),$$
the two-complex-dimensional analogue of the Witt algebra of holomorphic vector fields on the
punctured line. (When the theory lacks an unbroken $U(1)_R$ the relevant algebra is instead the
divergence-free $\mathfrak{svect}(2)$.) It is generated by the modes of a pair of semi-chiral
superfields $\mathbf S_i$ ($i=1,2$), coming from the $R$-current multiplet, which satisfy
$\bar\partial\mathbf S_i=0$.

Unlike the 2d Witt algebra, $\mathfrak{witt}(2)$ admits **no ordinary (2-cocycle) central
extension**. Instead it has a **two-dimensional space of $L_\infty$ central extensions**
(3-cocycles); turning them on gives the **2-Virasoro algebra** $\mathfrak{vir}(2)_{a,c}$, whose
two central parameters are precisely the 4d conformal anomalies $a$ and $c$.

## Motivation

In a 2d CFT the Virasoro central charge $c$ measures the Weyl anomaly through a 2-cocycle
(central extension) of the Witt algebra. The holomorphic twist of a 4d theory has *two* Weyl
anomalies, $a$ and $c$, and the appropriate home for both is a **single** infinite-dimensional
algebra. That the extensions are 3-cocycles (ternary brackets) rather than 2-cocycles is a
direct consequence of the derived nature of holomorphic symmetry in complex dimension $>1$.

## Key formulas

- Binary bracket (the $\mathfrak{witt}(2)$ structure):
  $\{\mathbf S_i{}_\lambda\mathbf S_j\}=\partial_i\mathbf S_j+\lambda_i\mathbf S_j+\lambda_j\mathbf S_i$.
- Central extension (the $a,c$ 3-cocycle):
  $\{\!\{\mathbf S_i\,\mathbf S_j\,\mathbf S_k\}\!\}
  =16(a-c)\,\mathcal I^{\rm vec}_{ijk}+\tfrac43(3c-2a)\,\mathcal I^{\mathcal N=4}_{ijk}$,
  where $\mathcal I^{\rm vec},\mathcal I^{\mathcal N=4}$ are the two invariant tensor structures.
- Geometric description of the extensions via the degree-four characteristic classes
  $(c_1^3,\,c_1 c_2)$ (Williams 2024).
