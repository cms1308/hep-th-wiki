## Definition

A **one-form global symmetry** is a global symmetry whose charged objects are line operators rather than local operators, and whose symmetry operators are codimension-2 (Gaiotto–Kapustin–Seiberg–Willett 2014). In a $2+1$-dimensional TQFT, an Abelian anyon $a$ of order $n$ ($a^n=\mathbf 1$) generates a $\mathbb Z_n^{(1)}$ one-form symmetry: the symmetry operator is the anyon line itself, and it measures the braiding phase of any line that pierces it. Gauging this symmetry is the field-theory meaning of condensing $a$.

## Motivation

Ordinary (zero-form) symmetries act on local operators and are gauged by summing over background gauge fields valued in the group. Higher-form symmetries extend this to extended operators, and are the natural language for topological order: ground-state degeneracy, anyons, and their fusion/braiding are all controlled by one-form symmetry and its anomaly. This viewpoint turns "anyon condensation" into "gauging a one-form symmetry," making its consistency conditions (anomalies) sharp.

## Key formulas

The **anomaly** of the $\mathbb Z_n^{(1)}$ generated by $a$ is set by the spin of $a$,
$$h(a)=\frac{p}{2n}\bmod 1,\qquad pn\in2\mathbb Z\ \text{(bosonic)}.$$
The symmetry is **anomaly-free** — hence gaugeable while preserving Lorentz invariance in $2+1$d — when $h(a)=0\bmod 1$, i.e. $p=0\bmod 2n$; the canonical anomaly-free representative is $p=0$. As a bare TQFT label $p$ is defined mod $2n$, but in the [[proliferation-transition|proliferation-transition]] construction of [[2603.00245|Cheng–Seiberg 2026]] the relevant object is the **integer lift** $p\in\mathbb Z$, which fixes the far-side theory and $L=\gcd(n,p)$.
