## Contributions

- One of the fathers of string theory (the hadronic-string interpretation of the Veneziano
  amplitude, ca. 1969–70).
- **Holographic principle** (1994–95, with 't Hooft's earlier dimensional-reduction idea):
  the proposal that a gravitating region's degrees of freedom live on its boundary at one
  per Planck area — a direct ancestor of the [[ads-cft-correspondence]].
- **Black hole complementarity** (with Thorlacius and Uglum, 1993): infalling and external
  descriptions of a black hole are complementary rather than contradictory; his side of the
  1990s [[black-hole-information-paradox]] debate against information loss.
- **Entropy renormalization**: the [[susskind-uglum-low-energy-theorem]] (with Uglum, 1994)
  — Rindler entropy per area equals $1/4G$ with the fully renormalized Newton constant.
- **Anti-remnant argument** ([[hep-th-9501106|Susskind 1995]]): information-storing
  [[black-hole-remnant|remnants]] would drive the renormalized $G$ to zero — closing the
  remnant branch of the information paradox.
- **Matrix theory** (BFSS, with Banks, Fischler and Shenker, 1996): a nonperturbative
  definition of M-theory in the infinite-momentum frame.
- Later work includes the anthropic/landscape framing of string vacua ("the anthropic
  landscape of string theory," 2003) and complexity/holography connections
  (computational complexity of black hole interiors, ER=EPR with Maldacena).

## Context

Felix Bloch Professor of Theoretical Physics at Stanford. Within this wiki he appears as
the central anti-remnant voice of the mid-1990s information-paradox debate: while
developing complementarity as the unitary alternative, he removed the competing remnant
resolution on thermodynamic grounds — the argument later borrowed by
[[hep-th-0601001|Arkani-Hamed-Motl-Nicolis-Vafa 2006]] as heuristic motivation for the
[[weak-gravity-conjecture]].
