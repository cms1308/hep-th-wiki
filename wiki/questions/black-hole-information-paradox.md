## Statement

Does the information carried by matter that falls into a black hole survive the black
hole's evaporation? Hawking radiation, in the original semiclassical calculation, is exactly
thermal and uncorrelated with the infallen state, so a black hole that evaporates completely
would evolve a pure state into a mixed state — in conflict with unitary quantum evolution.

## Why it matters

The paradox is a direct clash between the two pillars the semiclassical calculation is built
on — quantum field theory and general relativity — in their common regime of validity, and
is therefore a key constraint on any theory of quantum gravity: a proposed theory must say
where the information goes.

## Main attempts

The classic options, as they framed the mid-1990s debate:

- **Information loss**: evaporation is genuinely non-unitary (Hawking's original position).
- **Unitary evaporation**: the information comes out in subtle correlations in the Hawking
  radiation; in the 1990s this was developed into black hole complementarity
  ([[leonard-susskind|Susskind]] et al.) and later underwrote by the
  [[ads-cft-correspondence]], where evaporation is dual to manifestly unitary boundary
  dynamics.
- **Remnants**: evaporation halts at a Planck-scale [[black-hole-remnant|remnant]] that
  stores the information in an unbounded number of internal states
  (Aharonov–Casher–Nussinov 1987). Argued to be thermodynamically inconsistent by
  [[hep-th-9501106|Susskind 1995]]: the required unbounded species degeneracy drives the
  renormalized Newton constant to zero via the [[susskind-uglum-low-energy-theorem]].

## Current status

Open as posed, though the weight of evidence (AdS/CFT unitarity in particular) favors
unitary evaporation; the remnant branch is generally considered closed by the
thermodynamic pathology recorded on [[black-hole-remnant]]. The mechanism by which the
information escapes — and what happens to the interior — remains the active frontier
(this wiki does not yet cover the modern island/replica-wormhole developments; a GAP worth
filling).
