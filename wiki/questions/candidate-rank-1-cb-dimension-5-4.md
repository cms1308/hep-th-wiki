## Statement

An $SU(2)$ $N_f=2$ adjoint fixed point in the [[4d-n1-scft-landscape|landscape]] of
[[2408.02953|Cho-Maruyoshi-Nardoni-Song 2024]], with
$$W=\phi q_1^2+ M_1 q_2\tilde{q}_1 + M_2 q_2\tilde{q}_2 + \phi \tilde{q}_1\tilde{q}_2
+ X_1\mathrm{Tr}\,\phi^2 + \phi q_2\tilde{q}_1+ X_2 M_1\,,\qquad
(a,c)=\left(\tfrac{19}{48},\tfrac{5}{12}\right)\,,$$
passes *all* the superconformal-index conditions (necessary and sufficient) for
enhancement to $\mathcal{N}=2$ supersymmetry, with Coulomb branch index
$\mathcal{I}_C = \frac{1}{1-u^{5/4}}$ — i.e. a rank-1 theory with Coulomb branch
operator dimension $\Delta = \tfrac54$. But the geometric classification of rank-1
$\mathcal{N}=2$ SCFTs by their Coulomb branch geometry does **not** allow
$\Delta = \tfrac54$. Which gives: a loophole in the classification, or a spurious
landscape entry?

## Why it matters

Either resolution is significant: a genuine new rank-1 $\mathcal{N}=2$ SCFT would break
the geometric classification (a cornerstone of the $\mathcal{N}=2$ landscape); a
spurious entry would be a sharp, diagnosable example of an accidental symmetry invisible
to [[a-maximization]] plus index checks — exactly the failure mode that limits the
whole [[4d-n1-scft-landscape|deformation-classification program]].

## Main attempts

[[2408.02953|Cho-Maruyoshi-Nardoni-Song 2024]] compute the $\mathcal{N}=2$ index limits:
the Hall-Littlewood index sums to $\frac{1}{1-\mathfrak{t}}$, meaning the Higgs branch
would be $\mathbb{C}$ — not a cone, as a genuine $\mathcal{N}=2$ Higgs branch should
be. This hints at an unaccounted accidental symmetry, but no available tool can decide.
